# Figure captions, Paper 1

All figures are produced by `scripts/20_figures.py` (figure-studio `atlas` palette on white plotting panels; Times New Roman, bold), at 180 mm width and 600 dpi. Each figure is exported as PNG, TIFF, PDF and SVG in the corresponding sub-folder. Effects are differences in the probability of being killed or hospitalized, in percentage points, conditional on a police-reported injury crash.

## Figure 1. Methodological framework of the study

The study proceeds in six connected stages. (1) The four annual files of the French national police crash database (BAAC) for 2019–2025 are linked into one record per rider, giving an analysis sample of 46,920 riders involved in 44,817 crashes between 2020 and 2025. (2) Explanatory variables are ordered in four tiers according to the point at which they are determined, and the effect of a factor is adjusted only for variables in the same or earlier tiers. (3) Models are developed on crashes from 2020–2023 by cross-validation grouped by crash and are evaluated once on a locked test set of crashes from 2024–2025; crashes from 2019 are reserved for a sensitivity analysis. (4) A predictive branch (gradient boosting and logistic regression, explained with TreeSHAP) and a causal branch (double machine learning for thirteen pre-specified factors) are estimated in parallel. (5) The audit compares the SHAP contrast with the double machine learning estimate of each factor in sign, magnitude and rank. (6) The stability of the findings is examined through the sources of disagreement, group average effects and robustness checks. The numbers of records retained at each step of the sample derivation are given in Table 1.

## Figure 4. Dependence of data recording on injury severity

**(a–c)** Share of riders for whom each variable is recorded as unknown, among riders who were killed or hospitalized and among those slightly injured or unhurt, for bicycles, e-bikes and e-scooters. Values to the right of each pair are the differences in percentage points. **(d)** Difference in the unknown rate (severe minus non-severe) for the eighteen variables with the largest differences, by mode. Shaded bands indicate the pre-specified thresholds: variables differing by ten points or more were excluded from all models, and variables differing by five to ten points were examined in a sensitivity analysis. **(e)** Share of riders with safety equipment recorded as unknown, by year, severity and mode. **(f)** Share of riders with each variable recorded as unknown, by year, for all riders.

## Figure 5. Predictive performance and its dependence on the validation protocol

**(a)** Receiver operating characteristic curves and **(b)** precision–recall curves of the gradient-boosting model on the locked test set (2024–2025), for all riders and by mode. Legends report the area under each curve with a 95% confidence interval obtained by bootstrap resampling of crashes. **(c)** Calibration of the gradient-boosting and logistic regression models on the locked test set, by decile of predicted probability, with the calibration slope, calibration intercept and Brier score. **(d)** Distribution of the predicted probability among riders who were slightly injured or unhurt (left half) and killed or hospitalized (right half); horizontal lines mark medians, which are also printed above each pair, and vertical bars mark interquartile ranges. **(e)** Decision-curve analysis: net benefit of each model across threshold probabilities, compared with classifying all riders as severe. **(f)** Area under the ROC curve and **(g)** calibration slope of the gradient-boosting model under nine validation protocols with identical hyperparameters; *n* is the number of riders evaluated. Dashed lines mark the locked-test value in (f) and perfect calibration in (g).

## Figure 7. Audit of SHAP attributions against double machine learning estimates

**(a)** Four readings of each pre-specified factor for all riders: the normal approximation to the sampling distribution of the double machine learning (double ML) estimate with its 95% confidence interval (standard errors clustered by département), the SHAP contrast, the model what-if, and the unadjusted difference between the two groups. Asterisks mark a false discovery rate below 0.05. **(b)** SHAP contrast against the double ML estimate for 39 estimates (all riders and each mode); filled markers denote a false discovery rate below 0.05, the solid line is a least-squares fit to them, and the dashed line indicates equality. **(c)** SHAP contrast as a fraction of the double ML estimate. **(d)** Out-of-fold SHAP values of the defining variables in the two groups of each factor; vertical marks are group means. **(e)** Kendall rank correlation between the rankings of the twelve factors under each reading. **(f)** Difference between the SHAP contrast and the double ML estimate divided by the standard error of the latter; the band marks the 95% interval. The urban versus non-urban contrast is omitted because the overlap condition was not satisfied.

## Figure 8. Sources of disagreement between SHAP and double machine learning

**(a)** Double ML estimates under the pre-specified adjustment set (marker and shaded 95% confidence interval) and under adjustment for all variables, including those in later tiers (thin interval beneath); arrows show the shift between the two, and the values are given at the right. **(b)** Stepwise decomposition of the difference between the SHAP contrast and the double ML estimate: the first step is attributable to adjustment for later-tier variables (double ML with all controls minus double ML with the pre-specified set) and the second to the predictive model (SHAP contrast minus double ML with all controls). Diamonds and the right-hand column show the total difference. **(c)** SHAP contrast plotted against both double ML estimates for the twelve factors, with least-squares lines; gray links join the two estimates of the same factor and the dashed line indicates equality. **(d)** Mean absolute difference between each model-based reading and the two double ML estimates across the twelve factors, with the relative change.

## Figure 9. Effects by transport mode and group average effects

**(a)** Double ML estimates by factor and transport mode. Marker area is proportional to the absolute estimate; color indicates the direction of the effect; filled markers and asterisks denote a false discovery rate below 0.05. The estimate and its 95% confidence interval are printed beneath each marker. Estimates are omitted where either group contained fewer than 200 riders or 30 severe outcomes. **(b–d)** Group average effects for three factors. Wide bars are confidence intervals adjusted for multiple comparisons within each grouping variable (Bonferroni); narrow bars are pointwise 95% confidence intervals; *n* is the number of riders in each group. The dashed line marks the overall estimate and p-values test equality of the effect across the groups of each grouping variable. The grouping by opponent is conditional on a characteristic of the crash and is descriptive.

## Figure 10. Robustness of the double machine learning estimates

**(a)** Estimates under each robustness check, for the nine factors with a false discovery rate below 0.05 in the main analysis. The main estimate is given beneath each column heading. Cell color indicates the ratio of the estimate to the main estimate; a dagger marks estimates not significant at the 5% level and a dash marks checks that do not apply. The early-adopter check restricts both modes to the ten départements that recorded ten or more e-scooter riders in 2020. **(b)** Estimates and 95% confidence intervals for the e-scooter versus bicycle contrast under each check; open markers denote estimates not significant at the 5% level and the shaded band is the confidence interval of the main analysis. **(c)** Unadjusted difference and double ML estimate for the same contrast in all départements, in the ten early-adopter départements and in all other départements. **(d)** Absolute main estimate compared with the change in the estimate that would be produced by an unobserved confounder as strongly related to the factor and the outcome as département. RV denotes the robustness value.
